What should make you pause?

A message that claims there is a family emergency and asks for immediate financial help may be genuine—or it may be an attempt to bypass your judgment. Scammers often combine emotional pressure with urgency and an unusual request.

  • Urgency: “Do this right now” or “there is no time to call.”
  • Emotional pressure: a frightening story designed to trigger panic or guilt.
  • Unusual payment request: a request for a transfer, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or secrecy.
  • Unverified identity: a familiar display name or email signature is not proof of who sent it.

A safer response checklist

  1. Stop. Do not let the message's urgency set your pace.
  2. Verify independently. Contact the person through a number or channel you already trust—not details supplied in the message.
  3. Do not send money, gift cards, codes, or sensitive information until the request is confirmed.
  4. Report suspicious messages through your organization's approved security-reporting process.

Remember

When a request combines urgency, strong emotion, secrecy, and money, slow down and verify independently. If you already responded, contact your organization's IT or security team promptly.
